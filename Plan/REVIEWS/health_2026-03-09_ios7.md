## Greatest Risk
The same risk from the iOS6 brief remains and deepens: the iOS package now has 9+ service actors/components (APIClient, AuthService, ChatService, PushNotificationService, DeviceService, BiometricService, ApprovalService, KeychainService, plus ViewModels) that have never been instantiated together in a real app. iOS7 adds two more actors (BiometricService, ApprovalService) and a new dependency chain (ApprovalDetailViewModel depends on both ApprovalService AND BiometricService). Every phase adds more wiring that nobody has verified composes correctly. The first real app build in iOS11 is increasingly likely to surface initialization ordering, retain cycle, or actor reentrancy issues.

## Decisions Needed
- Consider creating a minimal App target before iOS11 to catch wiring issues early. The risk compounds with each additional phase.
- The backend approval endpoints are stubs (always returns [] for pending, always succeeds for decide). Decide whether to prioritize real backend approval persistence or accept that the full approval flow is untestable end-to-end until a future wave.

## Risks You Are Taking
1. **No app target**: 9+ service actors tested in isolation only. First real composition is iOS11. Medium probability of wiring failures, medium impact (fixable but costs a full debug cycle). This is the single biggest technical debt item.
2. **Backend approval stubs**: The entire approval flow (pending list, decide, batch) exercises only stub endpoints that return empty data or hardcoded success. The iOS code is structurally correct, but field mismatches or envelope format differences could emerge when real data flows. Low probability (CodingKeys are verified against backend models), medium impact.
3. **Stale refresh tokens**: AuthService.refresh() failure does not clear Keychain. A user with an expired refresh token will loop on 401 retries until the 3-attempt cap (then gets stuck). Low probability in normal use, low impact (user can log out manually), but degrades UX.
